Model and ranking method

A linear regression models pavement temperature from scenario air temperature, surface type, and shade. It estimates each site twice, using current and proposed shade. The planner chooses the combination that maximizes the sum of predicted pavement cooling × people per hour in newly shaded areas within the budget. The score ranks projects. The temperature estimate applies to pavement at each site.

ABOUT THE DATA

How the comparison works

Heat island estimate = urban mean air temperature − rural reference mean air temperature. Positive values indicate warmer urban air. The map shows 30-year weather-station normals.

Station locations are classified using 2020 Census urban boundaries. Each urban station is matched to its two or three nearest stations outside all urban areas, 5–80 km away and within 150 m of elevation. Each station needs at least 24 contributing years for annual, summer, and winter averages. The urban result averages the matched urban stations. The rural result averages their rural reference means, giving each urban station equal weight.

The reference file contains 457 qualifying Census urban areas. For example, Dallas–Fort Worth and Houston lack qualifying matches under these rules, so they are absent from the file. The app searches this fixed CSV and displays historical 1991–2020 averages. An area can have just one urban station. Terrain, coastal effects, and station siting can also affect the temperature gap. Station positions on the map represent the first matched urban station in each area.
